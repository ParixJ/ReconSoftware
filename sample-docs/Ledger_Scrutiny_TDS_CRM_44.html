Some AIS PDFs from the portal have no text layer (a "Print to PDF" export or a scan). This tool reads those using OCR (Tesseract), and OCR can occasionally misread a digit — e.g. reading ₹11,043 as ₹194. The Tax·AIS module no longer trusts a single OCR'd figure by itself. Here's what it does instead, and how to get the safest result:

StepWhat to doWhy
1In Tax · AIS · Advance tax, upload both the AIS/TIS PDF and Form 26AS, then click Run tax · AIS · advance tax review.Form 26AS is exported from TRACES as a native text PDF — it never needs OCR, so it's the more reliable of the two.
2For any Section 194A interest entry, the tool checks the amount against the TDS actually deducted, using the 10% statutory rate (TDS ≈ 10% of interest).Interest and TDS are independently printed figures — if OCR mangles one, it usually won't also mangle the other in a way that still satisfies the 10% relationship, so the mismatch gets caught.
3If AIS and 26AS agree, you'll see a Low finding: "AIS and Form 26AS agree — high confidence."Safe to book as-is.
4If they disagree, you'll see a High finding: "Interest income — MISMATCH," telling you to use the Form 26AS figure.26AS is treated as authoritative over an OCR'd AIS read.
5If only AIS was uploaded (no 26AS), you'll see "Interest income — unverified" at Medium or High.Medium = the TDS-rate check still passed; High = it couldn't be cross-validated at all — upload 26AS before relying on the number.

Tip: whenever the AIS read method note says "recovered via OCR," treat every amount on that page as unverified until it's backed by either the 10% cross-check or a matching Form 26AS entry — click the finding row to see the underlying evidence.

Advanced thresholds

Default ₹10,000. Cash payments above this are flagged (Sec 40A(3) / Rule 6DD). Separate from cash-in-hand balance.

Cash payments related to transport / freight / GTA above this are flagged (higher limit ₹30,000).

Cash-in-hand only — closing cash ledger balance above this is flagged. Distinct from cash receipt limits.

Cash receipts only — single cash receipt vouchers above this are flagged (Sec 269ST relevance). Separate from cash-in-hand.

Unsecured loan balances below this are flagged to query settling them.

Section 194I — rent ledger crossing this in any single month.

Section 194J — professional/consultancy fee ledger crossing this for the year.

RCM GST liability above this is checked for nullification (matching RCM ITC). Only non-nullified / partly nullified RCM is flagged High.

    MSME payment timeline (45-day rule)

    Under the MSME framework, buyers must clear invoices of Micro & Small suppliers within 15 days if there is no written agreement, and within a maximum of 45 days if a written agreement exists. This workspace splits MSME creditors into two lists: paid/settled within 45 days, and those paid after 45 days (or still unpaid beyond 45 days).

    Data source: Opens from your main ledger upload automatically (Sundry Creditors / supplier ledgers, MSME–Udyam hints). For exact invoice-wise 45-day maths, also upload a payment register with Invoice Date and Payment Date.

    1. Load MSME creditor data

    Automatic: click Detect from main ledger upload (or open this page after analysing books) to pull Sundry Creditor / supplier ledgers and split by the day limit. Optional file: Excel / CSV with Supplier, Udyam No., Invoice No., Invoice Date, Due Date, Payment Date, Outstanding Amount for invoice-level accuracy.

    Default 45 (written agreement max). Use 15 if no agreement.
